\begin{abstract}
This document is typeset in your browser by PhiTeX, an incremental TeX engine
compiled to WebAssembly. Change any word on the left: only the paragraph you
touch is typeset again, and the page on the right follows. The PDF is exactly
the one pdfLaTeX would make.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
TeX reads a whole document from the top every time it runs. PhiTeX keeps
what it built last time and reuses everything an edit cannot change, so a
keystroke costs a paragraph, not a document. The Gaussian integral is a
classic test of any typesetter:
\begin{equation}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}.
\end{equation}
Try changing the exponent, or add a second equation below it.

\section{See what you changed}
\label{sec:compare}
PhiTeX has latexdiff built in. To try it:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Change a few words in the paragraphs above, and delete a sentence.
  \item In the toolbar above the page, click \textbf{Compare versions} and choose
        \emph{As opened}. The page shows both versions at once, as latexdiff marks them:
        \textcolor{blue}{\underline{additions in blue}}, deletions
        \textcolor{red}{struck out in red}. Keep typing: the comparison follows.
\end{enumerate}
